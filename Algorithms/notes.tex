\section{Solved Exercises}
\subsection*{Exercise 1}
Consider a town with n men and n women seeking to get married to one
another. Each man has a preference list that ranks all the women, and
each woman has a preference list that ranks all the men.  The set of
all 2n people is divided into two categories: good people and bad
people. Suppose that for some number k, 1= k = n - 1, there are k good
men and k good women; thus there are n - k bad men and n - k bad
women.  Everyone would rather marry any good person than any bad
person.  Formally, each preference list has the property that it ranks
each good person of the opposite gender higher than each bad person of
the opposite gender: its first k entries are the good people (of the
opposite gender) in some order, and its next n - k are the bad people
(of the opposite gender) in some order.  Show that in every stable
matching, every good man is married to a good woman.\\
\textbf{Solution} (By contradiction)\\
Let's suppose we get a stable matching \textbf{S} in which a certain
good man \textbf{m} is married to a bad woman \textbf{w'}. That
implies there's a certain good woman \textbf{w} that is married to a
bad man \textbf{m'}, since there is the same amount of good men as
good women (k). That means we have an instability, since \textbf{m}
prefers \textbf{w} over \textbf{w'}, and \textbf{w} prefers \textbf{m}
over \textbf{m'}. This scenario can only happen in two cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{m} never proposed to \textbf{w}. However, this cannnot happen, since \textbf{w} is ranked higher than \textbf{w'} in \textbf{m}'s preference list.
\item \textbf{w} rejected \textbf{m}. Once again, this is not possible, since \textbf{m} is ranked higher than \textbf{m'} in \textbf{w}'s preference list.
\end{enumerate}
We have found a contradiction, so \textbf{m, w'} could have never
match to begin with.
